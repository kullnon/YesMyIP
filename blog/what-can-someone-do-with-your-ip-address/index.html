What Can Someone Do With Your IP Address?

YesMyIP Editorial Team
November 27, 2026 · 6 min read

Your IP address is visible to every website, app and online service you connect to. That is how the internet works: a reply cannot reach you unless the other side knows where to send it. So it is fair to ask what a stranger could actually do with that number. The short answer is less than most people fear, but not nothing. This guide separates the real risks from the myths.

What Your IP Address Reveals

A public IP address is assigned to your connection by your internet provider. Blocks of addresses are registered to providers in public databases, which is why anyone can look up a few basic facts about one. You can see what yours shows on the homepage, or check any address with the IP lookup tool.

The location is an estimate drawn from those databases. It often points to the right city, and sometimes to a different one where your provider has its equipment.

What Someone Can Do With Your IP Address

See roughly where you are

Anyone who has your address can look up the provider and the approximate area. Combined with other things you have shared, such as a username or a photo, that can help a determined person narrow down who you are. On its own it does not lead to your door.

Block or restrict you

Websites, games and streaming services use IP addresses to decide what to show and whom to let in. An address can be banned from a forum or a game server, and content can be limited to certain countries based on where the address appears to be.

Recognize you across visits

Sites and advertising networks can note that the same address came back, and use it alongside cookies and other signals to connect your visits. Because one home address is shared by everyone on the same Wi-Fi, this identifies a household more than a person. The difference is explained in public vs private IP address.

Flood your connection

An attacker can direct a large amount of junk traffic at an address so that the connection slows down or drops. This is called a denial-of-service attack. It is most often aimed at servers and businesses, and occasionally at individuals, for example during a dispute in an online game. It disrupts the connection but does not give the attacker access to your devices.

Probe your router

Automated scanners sweep across the internet looking for devices that answer on open ports. They do this to every address, not only yours. A home router normally refuses connections that nothing inside your network asked for, so a scan finds something only if a service has been opened to the internet or the router has a known weakness that was never patched.

What Someone Cannot Do With Your IP Address

  • Find your name or exact address. The record linking an address to a customer is held by your internet provider and is not published.
  • Get into your devices just by knowing it. An address is a destination, not a key. Breaking in also requires a weakness, such as an exposed service or a default password.
  • Read your messages or see what you browse. Knowing where your connection is does not let an outsider see the traffic passing through it.
  • Log in to your accounts. Accounts are protected by passwords and sign-in checks, not by your IP address.

How Someone Gets Your IP Address

No special skill is needed, because sharing the address is part of connecting:

  • Visiting a website or opening a link. The server on the other end sees the address the request came from.
  • Peer-to-peer apps. File-sharing programs, and some games and calling apps, connect users directly to each other, so the people you connect with can see your address.
  • Email. Some mail setups include the sender's address in the hidden headers of a message.
  • Sharing a network. Anyone who uses your Wi-Fi goes online with the same public address as you.

How to Protect Your IP Address

A few habits cover most of the risk:

  1. Keep your router updated and replace its default admin password with one of your own.
  2. Do not open ports you do not need. Turn off remote administration and port forwarding rules you are not using.
  3. Be careful with links from strangers, since opening one shows your address to whoever runs the page.
  4. Get a new address if yours is being targeted. Most home connections have a dynamic address, and leaving the router off for a while or asking your provider can change it. See static vs dynamic IP address for how that works.
  5. Use a VPN. A VPN sends your traffic through an encrypted tunnel to a server run by the VPN service, so websites, games and peer-to-peer contacts see that server's address instead of yours. Services such as NordVPN and Surfshark work this way.

The Bottom Line

Your IP address shows your provider and your approximate area, and it gives others a way to block, recognize or pester your connection. It does not reveal your name, your home address or anything stored on your devices. Keep your router in good shape, think before opening links from strangers, and use a VPN when you would rather keep your real address to yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can someone find my home address from my IP address?

Not on their own. A public IP address points to your internet provider and an approximate area, usually a city or region. The record that links an address to a customer is held by the provider and is not published.

Can someone hack my computer with just my IP address?

Knowing the address is not enough. An attacker would also need a weakness to use, such as an exposed service, outdated router software or a default password. A home router normally refuses connections from the internet that nothing on your network asked for.

Is it dangerous if someone knows my IP address?

For most people the risk is low. Every website and online service you use sees your IP address as a normal part of connecting. The realistic problems are approximate location, blocking, tracking across visits and, less often, unwanted traffic aimed at your connection.

How do I stop people from seeing my IP address?

Use a VPN, which sends your traffic through its own server so that websites and apps see the server's address instead of yours. If someone already has your address and it is a dynamic one, restarting your router or asking your provider can get you a new one.
